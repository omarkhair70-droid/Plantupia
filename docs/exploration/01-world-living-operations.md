# World 01 — Living Operations

Status: EXPLORATION / NOT A PRODUCT DECISION  
Date: 2026-10-07

## Provisional thesis

A commercial green space should behave like a living operational asset rather than a finished installation.

The useful object is not "a list of plants."

It is a persistent relationship:

site → zone → living entities → observations → interventions → outcomes → evidence.

## Relationship this world changes

Today, design intent, installation reality, field-service observations, client reporting, and future decisions can easily become separate information systems or disappear into people, chats, spreadsheets, and photos.

This world asks:

> What if the living space itself kept the record?

## Primary experience

The interface is quiet.

A facility manager does not open a wall of 300 plant cards.

The default state is:

> Everything is quiet. Three things deserve attention.

A technician opens a zone and sees:

- what changed
- what happened last time
- why the previous decision was made
- what should be observed before acting
- the evidence behind the recommendation

The client sees meaningful service proof rather than operational noise.

## Product behavior

- context before advice
- changes before raw readings
- exception-first interface
- observation → decision → intervention → result
- role-specific views over one shared record
- measured / observed / inferred separated visibly
- history remains when staff changes

## Physical / digital relationship

Primarily digital, but grounded in physical spaces.

Possible physical bridges may include:

- QR / NFC
- photography
- environmental sensors
- floor plans
- technician observations

None is core by itself.

## Possible business

1. Included inside Plantupia recurring maintenance / managed-space contracts.
2. Client-facing proof layer that increases perceived recurring-service value.
3. Later, a professional SaaS layer for interiorscapers or living-space operators.

## Why Plantupia may have a right to build it

A pure SaaS company can build field-service software.

Plantupia's potential advantage would only exist if the system is shaped by repeated exposure to:

- real installations
- real plant failures and recoveries
- real maintenance work
- real spatial design decisions
- real client expectations

Without that feedback loop, this becomes generic operations software.

## Direct references

### ORTH
https://www.askorth.com/

Useful principle:
- one persistent plot record
- every decision / action stays with the plot
- teams with different roles act on one source of truth
- retained context makes later intelligence stronger

Do not copy:
- agriculture workflows literally
- chat as the default interaction

### One Big Green
https://www.onebiggreen.com/

Useful baseline:
- plant / site inventory
- maintenance records
- field reporting
- visible client proof

Important lesson:
These are category plumbing, not sufficient differentiation.

## Lateral references

### Operational digital twins / facility systems
Useful principle:
A representation matters only if real operational change can update it.

### Calm technology
Useful principle:
The system should demand attention only when a meaningful exception exists.

## What this world must not become

- a generic CRM
- a technician timesheet product
- 300 green status cards
- "digital twin" as a 3D visual gimmick
- sensor dashboards with no decision value
- AI that invents certainty from incomplete context

## Smallest useful experiment

Use one real or simulated commercial site with:

- 1 site
- 3 zones
- 8–12 living entities
- 2 service visits
- 1 recurring problem
- 1 intervention and later result

Prototype only the loop:

observe → interpret → act → return → compare.

Success question:

> Does the record make the next human decision materially better?

## Kill conditions

Kill or demote this world if:

- Plantupia's real operation has no recurring information-loss problem
- generic field-service software already solves the valuable part adequately
- clients do not value visible continuity / proof
- the system requires excessive documentation from technicians
