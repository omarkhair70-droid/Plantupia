Attack the four exploration worlds before selecting one.

# World 01 — Living Operations

## Strongest case

This is closest to an existing commercial operation.

Interiorscaping software already proves that recurring plant-service companies need:

- site / asset inventory
- service scheduling
- field reporting
- replacements
- maintenance history
- job / account economics

One Big Green already covers much of this baseline.

Therefore the opportunity is not "build operations software."

The potentially differentiated layer is:

> persistent living context + meaningful change + intervention history + evidence

instead of generic field-service administration.

## Why it could win

Plantupia can potentially observe the full loop:

design → installation → service → stress / failure → intervention → result.

If that loop is retained, later decisions can improve from real outcomes rather than generic care rules.

## Main contradiction

The closer this world gets to scheduling, billing, crew tracking, CRM and work orders, the less differentiated it becomes.

The strongest business need may pull the product toward the least distinctive product.

## Adoption risk

Field teams punish documentation friction.

If technicians must record too much, data quality collapses.

The system must therefore extract value from very small observations.

## Technical risk

Moderate.

The hardest problem is not coding the records.

It is:

- defining useful context
- maintaining data quality
- handling incomplete observations
- making history useful rather than noisy
- deciding which signals actually matter

## Business risk

Existing specialized software already sells to this category.

One Big Green currently markets plant inventory, installation / maintenance workflows, service reports, profitability and scheduling directly to interiorscapers.

A Plantupia-built full FSM would need a very strong reason to exist.

## Current verdict

**KEEP, but narrow.**

Do not build a full operations suite first.

Test the smallest unique layer:

> Does retained living context improve the next field decision?

Potential strategic role:
backbone / context layer rather than standalone "all-in-one software."

# World 02 — Plant Literacy

## Strongest case

This world has the clearest human thesis:

> technology should make the user better at noticing the living thing.

That is meaningfully different from reminder-first design.

A strong lateral precedent exists in Merlin Bird ID:

- the model gives possibilities
- uncertainty is accepted
- the human confirms
- the tool is used to learn perceptual skill, not only output an answer

Cornell explicitly encourages users to use Merlin to learn, investigate suggestions and accept uncertainty rather than blindly trusting the top result.

## Why it could win

If done correctly, it can express nearly all current Plantupia principles:

- context
- time
- memory
- care
- uncertainty
- legibility
- evidence

It is also globally distributable without physical installation.

## Main contradiction

A product that succeeds by making the user more independent may reduce its own dependency loop.

That is philosophically attractive but creates a retention / subscription question.

The product must remain valuable after basic plant literacy improves.

Possible long-term value might come from:

- harder interpretation
- longitudinal history
- changing environments
- new plants
- shared care
- advanced learning

But this is unproven.

## Right-to-win risk

Consumer plant care is crowded.

Current products already compete on:

- reminders
- identification
- diagnosis
- schedules
- conversational advice
- plant history
- home / light context

Therefore "better advice" is not enough.

Plantupia would need unique knowledge, data, teaching method, or interaction model.

## Technical risk

Low to moderate for a prototype.

High if the thesis depends on reliable diagnosis from images.

The product should avoid making computer vision accuracy the core proof.

## Business risk

High.

Consumer subscription acquisition and retention may become much harder than building the software.

A psychologically elegant product is not automatically a viable consumer business.

## Current verdict

**KEEP AS A DISTINCT GLOBAL RESEARCH LANE, but do not assume it is the first commercial product.**

It may become:
- a future global product
- a learning layer across other products
- a design philosophy that influences B2B technician tools

# World 03 — Living Object

## Strongest case

This is the clearest synthesis of:

art × spatial design × living material × technology × product.

It gives Plantupia an opportunity to create a recognizable physical category rather than another software surface.

Naava proves that physical greenery, maintenance and technology can coexist commercially.

Green Furniture Concept shows that planting and furniture can become one modular spatial system.

Calm-technology references strengthen the interaction thesis:

- minimum necessary attention
- communicate through the periphery
- minimum technology needed
- remain useful when technology fails

## Why it could win

A pure software company cannot easily reproduce:

- physical design
- installation
- living-system care
- service operations
- spatial authorship

This gives the world a stronger possible right-to-win.

## Main contradiction

The world's creative strength can become its business weakness.

Every physical decision adds:

- manufacturing
- sourcing
- shipping
- installation
- maintenance
- replacement
- water / electrical safety
- cleaning
- reliability
- after-sales support

A beautiful prototype can hide terrible lifecycle economics.

## Technical risk

Potentially high, but avoidable during discovery.

Do not manufacture custom electronics first.

Use off-the-shelf sensing until the interaction value is proven.

## Interaction risk

Ambient communication is not automatically legible.

A subtle glowing object may be aesthetically excellent and operationally useless.

Calm technology only works if the signal can move from periphery to attention at the correct moment.

## Failure-mode requirement

The object must remain a good plant / furniture / spatial object when:

- sensor fails
- Wi-Fi fails
- app disappears
- cloud service stops
- battery dies

Technology cannot be a single point of failure for basic care.

## Business risk

High initial capital / operational complexity.

But recurring care may create a stronger model than one-off object sales.

## Current verdict

**KEEP AS THE STRONGEST CREATIVE / PHYSICAL R&D WORLD.**

Do not treat it as the first production commitment.

Its first job is to prove a relationship:
can a living object communicate meaningful state without becoming a gadget?

# World 04 — Living Materials / Specifier Infrastructure

## Strongest case

This world enters before procurement.

BIMobject's current manufacturer proposition is explicitly about being present in architects' workflows at product-selection time.

Its 2025–2026 material states that downloadable BIM objects are increasingly important to specifiers and that product data embedded in design workflows influences earlier selection.

Naava already provides architects and designers with downloadable objects across Revit, Archicad, AutoCAD, Rhino and SketchUp plus design support.

This validates the channel.

## Why it could win

Plantupia already belongs to a physical category that architects and interior designers can influence.

The distinctive opportunity is not "make BIM files."

It is:

> connect spatial intent and biological viability before specification, then keep the same identity alive after installation.

This can reduce the discontinuity between:

concept → specification → installation → maintenance.

## Main contradiction

This world requires something stable enough to specify.

If Plantupia's work is almost entirely bespoke and every solution is reinvented per project, a rich configurator / object library may be premature.

The digital layer cannot standardize a product system that the physical business has not yet standardized.

## Technical risk

Moderate.

A useful first product does not require building a CAD application.

The simplest valuable layer may be:

- reliable assets
- technical data
- spatial / biological selection logic
- design support
- RFQ continuity

## Adoption risk

Architects will reject a separate tool if it creates work.

The product must enter existing tools / habits rather than demand a Plantupia-only design universe.

## Business risk

This may work brilliantly as a sales / distribution layer while being weak as standalone SaaS.

That is not necessarily a problem.

Its value may be:

- earlier project entry
- higher specification rate
- less sales friction
- more standardized delivery
- higher-quality handoff

## Current verdict

**KEEP, with a major prerequisite check: does Plantupia have repeatable systems worth specifying?**

If yes, this may have very strong near-term leverage.

If no, first standardize the physical offering / specification logic rather than overbuilding software.

# Collision 01 — World 01 and World 04 may be two halves of one lifecycle

World 04 starts before installation:

intent → selection → specification.

World 01 starts during / after installation:

as-built → observation → intervention → result.

The strongest collision is:

> Why should these be two records?

A specified living system could become the as-built living identity after installation.

Then field reality feeds back into future specification.

Possible loop:

design intent  
→ living specification  
→ approved configuration  
→ installed reality  
→ care history  
→ performance / failure evidence  
→ better future specification

This is more interesting than either a BIM library or maintenance app alone.

---

# Collision 02 — World 02 may be a behavior layer, not only a consumer product

Plant Literacy may apply to:

- consumers
- technicians
- facility teams
- architects

Examples:

A technician can be prompted to observe before blindly following a schedule.

An architect can learn why a living system fits one environmental condition and fails in another.

A facility manager can learn which visible changes deserve escalation.

Therefore:

> "teach people to notice" may be a cross-product interaction law.

It may still become a consumer product, but it is not limited to that world.

---

# Collision 03 — World 03 may need World 01 / 04 underneath it

A Living Object without lifecycle context risks becoming a gadget.

Its stronger version may require:

- specification context
- installation identity
- service history
- living-state change
- maintenance access
- evidence

In that case World 03 is not a separate digital universe.

It is a physical expression of a deeper living-system record.

# Emergent hypothesis — a fifth world is appearing

Working name only:

## Living Layer

Not an app.
Not a dashboard.
Not a smart planter.
Not a BIM library.

A continuity layer for living systems across their lifecycle:

intent  
→ context  
→ specification  
→ installation  
→ care  
→ change  
→ evidence  
→ learning.

Different surfaces may sit on top:

- architect / designer surface
- technician surface
- client surface
- consumer learning surface
- ambient physical object
- future intelligence

The important claim is not that Plantupia must build all surfaces.

The hypothesis is:

> The same living identity and retained context may be more valuable than any individual interface.

This is not yet the product thesis.

It is the first cross-world pattern strong enough to deserve its own next research test.

# Immediate next gates

Before selecting a world, answer these reality questions with Plantupia:

1. What does the current project lifecycle actually look like from lead to maintenance?
2. Where is information currently lost between sales, design, installation and care?
3. Does Plantupia have recurring maintenance today? At what scale?
4. How standardized versus bespoke are current installations?
5. Who currently chooses / specifies Plantupia in commercial projects?
6. Do architects / designers materially influence the sale?
7. What happens when a plant fails after installation?
8. What data or photos already exist after handover?
9. Which activities are expensive, repetitive or invisible to the client?
10. What does the team currently wish it knew but does not know?

These answers may kill, merge or reorder the worlds.

## Next research / experiment step

Do not build four prototypes.

First run the **Living Layer hypothesis** against one real Plantupia project lifecycle.

Map:

brief → design → approval → procurement → installation → handover → service → issue → intervention → outcome.

Mark:

- information created
- information lost
- decision owner
- physical artifact
- repeated manual work
- evidence gap
- opportunity for living continuity

Only then decide which prototype has the highest information value.
