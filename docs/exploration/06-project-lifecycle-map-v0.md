# Step 05B — Plantupia Project Lifecycle Map v0

Status: HYPOTHESIS MAP / NEEDS COMPANY CHECK  
Date: 2026-10-07

## Purpose

Map one Plantupia commercial project from first contact through ongoing care.

This is deliberately not treated as company truth.

Every stage is marked as one of:

- **KNOWN (PUBLIC)** — supported by public information already reviewed
- **ASSUMED** — inferred from category / business logic
- **NEEDS COMPANY CHECK** — important unknown that must be verified directly

The purpose is to locate:

- where information is created
- where it may disappear
- where people repeat work
- where design intent can be lost
- where care becomes invisible
- where a persistent living identity could create value
- what must be validated before building anything

---

# Lifecycle overview

Lead / opportunity  
→ discovery / brief  
→ site understanding  
→ concept / planting design  
→ proposal / approval  
→ procurement / preparation  
→ installation  
→ handover  
→ ongoing care / maintenance  
→ issue / intervention  
→ outcome / replacement / recovery  
→ renewal / expansion / future design learning

This sequence is a working model, not confirmed Plantupia process.

# Stage 01 — Lead / Opportunity

Status: **KNOWN (PUBLIC) + NEEDS COMPANY CHECK**

Publicly supported:
Plantupia works with commercial spaces such as offices, hospitality and similar environments.

Likely inputs:
- client / company
- project type
- location
- approximate scope
- contact person
- project timing

### Key unknowns

- Where do leads come from today?
- Direct outreach?
- referrals?
- architects / interior designers?
- fit-out contractors?
- social / inbound?
- repeat clients?

### Why this matters

The answer affects whether an architect / specifier product could create meaningful distribution leverage.

### Potential information-loss point

If early client intent exists only in sales chat / calls and never becomes structured project context, later design decisions may lose the original reason for the project.

# Stage 02 — Discovery / Brief

Status: **ASSUMED / NEEDS COMPANY CHECK**

Possible questions:
- What should the space feel like?
- What business / operational function does greenery serve?
- Is the goal decorative, privacy, zoning, arrival, hospitality atmosphere, wellbeing, branding, or another outcome?
- Budget?
- maintenance expectation?
- deadline?
- client preferences / restrictions?

### Critical distinction

The brief may contain two different truths:

1. **Aesthetic intent**
2. **Operational / biological reality**

Both need to survive.

### Potential Living Layer object

**Design Intent**

Possible fields:
- desired spatial effect
- desired visual language
- operational constraint
- maintenance tolerance
- client priority
- confidence / source

### Needs company check

- Is a formal brief created today?
- Who creates it?
- Where does it live?
- Does it survive into installation / maintenance?

# Stage 03 — Site Understanding

Status: **ASSUMED / HIGH-PRIORITY COMPANY CHECK**

Potential inputs:
- floor plan
- photographs
- dimensions
- natural / artificial light
- HVAC exposure
- temperature / humidity conditions
- traffic / touch
- access for care
- water availability
- drainage constraints
- safety / cleaning constraints
- opening hours / guest or employee patterns

### Core research relationship

> plant-in-context before plant selection

### Potential evidence types

- measured
- observed
- estimated
- client-reported
- design-assumed

These should not be silently treated as equivalent.

### Potential information-loss point

A designer may make a decision from a site observation that never reaches the person maintaining the installation later.

Example:

> "This plant was selected here because direct afternoon sun is blocked by the facade."

If that knowledge disappears, later care may treat the plant generically.

### Needs company check

- Is there a site survey?
- Who performs it?
- Which measurements are taken?
- Are light conditions actually measured or visually estimated?
- Is the floor plan available?
- Are photos organized?
- Does anyone revisit assumptions after installation?

# Stage 04 — Concept / Living-Space Design

Status: **KNOWN AT CATEGORY LEVEL / NEEDS COMPANY CHECK**

Potential outputs:
- zones
- plant / system selection
- planter / container selection
- spatial composition
- quantities
- visual direction
- placement
- maintenance access assumptions
- expected growth / future form

### Important research law

The unit should not automatically be:

> species selected for a point

It may be:

> living system selected to perform a spatial role under specific conditions

Possible spatial roles:
- focal point
- soft boundary
- privacy layer
- transition
- human-scale correction
- hospitality atmosphere
- low-maintenance background
- living divider

### Potential Living Layer object

**Living Specification**

It could retain:
- design intent
- context assumptions
- selected living system
- why it fits
- care implications
- access requirements
- evidence / confidence

### Needs company check

- Who designs?
- What tools are used?
- Is the design 2D, 3D, moodboard, spreadsheet, WhatsApp, CAD?
- How much is standardized vs bespoke?
- Does Plantupia have repeatable modules / systems?
- Does an architect / interior designer approve or influence the choice?

# Stage 05 — Proposal / Approval

Status: **ASSUMED / NEEDS COMPANY CHECK**

Potential outputs:
- concept approval
- quantities
- cost
- maintenance options
- installation scope
- timeline
- exclusions
- replacement policy
- recurring service terms

### Potential discontinuity

Commercial approval may simplify a rich design into line items.

Example:

Design intent:
> create a semi-private arrival zone with a living vertical layer

Commercial line:
> 8 planters + 24 plants

If only the commercial line survives, the reason behind the composition disappears.

### Needs company check

- What exactly does the client approve?
- Who signs off?
- What changes during negotiation?
- Does value engineering change plant / planter / layout decisions?
- Are those changes recorded with reasons?

# Stage 06 — Procurement / Preparation

Status: **ASSUMED / NEEDS COMPANY CHECK**

Potential reality:
- availability changes
- supplier substitutions
- size / maturity differs
- container changes
- timing affects stock
- cost changes

### Critical question

Does the installed system remain faithful to the approved specification?

Or does procurement silently alter it?

### Potential Living Layer transition

**Specified → Approved → Procured**

Each should be distinguishable.

### Needs company check

- How often are substitutions necessary?
- Who approves them?
- Is actual procured material documented?
- Are plant sizes / conditions recorded before delivery?

# Stage 07 — Installation

Status: **KNOWN (PUBLIC) + NEEDS COMPANY CHECK**

Publicly supported:
Plantupia installs commercial green spaces.

Potential installation realities:
- final location adjustments
- site conditions differ from plan
- quantities change
- physical access changes
- plant orientation changes
- planters move
- client requests last-minute edits

### Highest-priority continuity question

> Is there an as-built record after installation?

The project may leave design intent and become a real living environment here.

### Potential Living Layer object

**As-Built Living Identity**

Could contain:
- exact installed location
- installed entity / system
- install date
- baseline photo
- actual planter / substrate
- actual site condition
- substitutions
- changed assumptions
- responsible care plan

### Needs company check

- Is anything tagged / numbered?
- Are baseline photos taken?
- Is exact placement recorded?
- Who owns the final truth after installation?

# Stage 08 — Handover

Status: **ASSUMED / NEEDS COMPANY CHECK**

Possible handover:
- client receives completed space
- care responsibility transfers or continues with Plantupia
- instructions / service terms communicated

### Critical fork

There may be two business models:

A. **Install and leave**
B. **Install and remain responsible**

The product opportunity changes radically depending on which is real.

### Needs company check

- What percentage of projects include recurring maintenance?
- Is maintenance optional or central?
- Who becomes responsible for daily care?
- What does the client receive at handover?
- Does Plantupia retain ownership / responsibility for any components?

# Stage 09 — Ongoing Care / Maintenance

Status: **ASSUMED / VERY HIGH-PRIORITY COMPANY CHECK**

Potential service actions:

- inspection
- pruning
- cleaning
- pest / disease observation
- soil / substrate attention
- rotation
- fertilization
- replacement recommendation
- planter / irrigation inspection
- photography
- issue escalation

### Current research hypothesis

The valuable unit may not be:

> technician completed visit

It may be:

> What changed in the living system, what did we observe, and what decision follows?

### Desired low-friction record

Observation  
→ interpretation  
→ intervention  
→ expected result  
→ next comparison

### Needs company check

- Are visits scheduled?
- On what cadence?
- Who performs them?
- What do they record today?
- Are photos taken?
- Does the same technician revisit the same project?
- Where do notes go?
- What information do technicians wish they had before arrival?
- What information do they hate entering?

# Stage 10 — Issue / Stress / Failure

Status: **ASSUMED / HIGH-PRIORITY COMPANY CHECK**

Examples:
- leaf decline
- pest
- water stress
- unsuitable light
- HVAC stress
- client overwatering
- physical damage
- irrigation issue
- plant mortality
- visual decline
- planter problem

### Critical distinction

A problem may come from:

- plant biology
- original specification
- changed site conditions
- maintenance quality
- client behavior
- procurement substitution
- system / hardware failure

Without history, these causes can look identical.

### Living Layer opportunity

An issue should connect backward to:

- original intent
- site assumptions
- actual installed condition
- previous observations
- previous interventions

### Needs company check

- How are issues reported?
- By client or technician?
- Who decides the response?
- How often is root cause actually known?
- Is a failed plant simply replaced, or is the reason investigated?

# Stage 11 — Intervention

Status: **ASSUMED / NEEDS COMPANY CHECK**

Possible intervention:
- watering change
- relocate
- prune
- treat pest
- replace
- change plant species
- adjust planter
- adjust irrigation
- modify light
- client behavior correction

### Important product law

Intervention is not closure.

The system should preserve:

> what was changed + why + what should happen next

### Needs company check

- Who chooses intervention?
- Is reason recorded?
- Is expected outcome recorded?
- Is follow-up automatic, scheduled, or memory-based?

# Stage 12 — Outcome / Recovery / Replacement

Status: **ASSUMED / NEEDS COMPANY CHECK**

This stage is essential because it closes the learning loop.

Possible outcomes:
- recovered
- partially improved
- no change
- worsened
- replaced
- relocated
- client accepted decline
- design changed

### Current research law

> trajectory before snapshot

A recommendation becomes knowledge only when its later result is known.

### Potential information-loss point

If replacement closes the ticket without preserving failure reason, the organization never learns.

### Needs company check

- Is outcome ever recorded?
- Do teams compare before / after?
- Are recurring failures identifiable across projects?
- Does a design choice get revised because of maintenance evidence?

# Hypothesized handoff breaks

These are not confirmed problems.

They are questions to test.

## Break A — Sales → Design

Is business intent translated into usable spatial / care intent?

## Break B — Design → Procurement

Are substitutions visible and approved?

## Break C — Procurement → Installation

Is the actual installed system documented?

## Break D — Installation → Maintenance

Does the technician know why the system was designed that way?

## Break E — Maintenance → Client

Does the client see meaningful care value?

## Break F — Maintenance → Future Design

Do failures and recoveries improve later specification?

The strongest Living Layer case would exist if several of these breaks are real.

# Reality-check interview — minimum useful questions

These are intentionally operational, not abstract product questions.

1. Walk me through the last real project from first client message until today.
2. What files / tools / chats existed at each stage?
3. Who created the design?
4. What changed between the first design and what was installed?
5. Where was that change written?
6. After installation, what information did the maintenance person receive?
7. What does a maintenance visit actually look like minute by minute?
8. What gets photographed or written down?
9. When a plant is declining, how do you decide why?
10. If you replace it, do you later know whether the replacement solved the real cause?
11. What repeated task wastes the most time?
12. What information do you repeatedly ask someone else for?
13. What does the client ask about after handover?
14. What work do you do that the client rarely notices?
15. What decision today is mostly experience / intuition because the data is missing?
16. Which project mistake do you see repeating?
17. Does an architect / interior designer influence selection before Plantupia enters?
18. How much of your solution is reusable from project to project?
19. What would you never want software to make slower?
20. If you could remember one thing perfectly across every project, what would it be?

# Observation protocol for one real project

Do not begin by asking the team to imagine ideal software.

Choose one actual project.

Collect only the artifacts that already exist:

- client brief / message
- site photos
- floor plan
- design / proposal
- approved scope
- procurement changes
- installation photos
- maintenance notes / chats
- service photos
- issue history
- replacement history

Then reconstruct the project as a timeline.

For every item, mark:

- SOURCE
- OWNER
- CREATED WHEN
- USED BY WHOM
- LOST / RETAINED
- TRUST LEVEL
- DECISION IT AFFECTED

This becomes evidence for or against the Living Layer.

---

# Stop conditions

Do not prototype the Living Layer merely because the lifecycle map looks elegant.

Stop / rethink if:

- the actual project lifecycle is much simpler
- maintenance is not recurring / strategically important
- information loss is low
- existing tools already preserve the valuable context
- technicians would have to perform unreasonable documentation
- most decisions are too bespoke to learn from
- clients / designers do not benefit from continuity
