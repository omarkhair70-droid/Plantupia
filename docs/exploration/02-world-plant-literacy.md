# World 02 — Plant Literacy

Status: EXPLORATION / NOT A PRODUCT DECISION  
Date: 2026-10-07

## Provisional thesis

The strongest consumer product may not be an app that cares for plants on behalf of people.

It may be a product that teaches people to notice living change.

> Help me read the plant, not ignore it.

## Relationship this world changes

Most plant-care software is tempted to optimize for:

identify → diagnose → remind → tell user exactly what to do.

This world asks a different question:

> Can technology make a person more plant-literate over time?

The product becomes a field guide for the specific living things the user already knows.

## Primary experience

The user sees a change.

The system does not immediately say:

"Water 340 ml now."

It may ask:

- What changed since last week?
- Touch the top layer of soil. Is it dry below the surface?
- Is the leaf soft, crisp, yellowing, or curling?
- Has the light in this location changed?
- Compare with this photo from 12 days ago.

Then the system may offer a cautious interpretation with evidence and uncertainty.

Over time, the user should need the product less for basic observations and more for difficult interpretation.

## Product behavior

- observation before command
- comparison over time
- teach visual / tactile cues
- preserve meaningful physical care
- confidence shown instead of false certainty
- advice grounded in the user's plant history and context
- growth / recovery remain the reward, not artificial gamification

## Physical / digital relationship

The screen should send attention back toward the plant.

Possible inputs:

- camera
- simple questions
- touch / soil observation
- location / light context
- optional sensor data

The product should not require hardware to be useful.

## Possible business

Potential global consumer subscription, but only if the relationship is differentiated enough to justify payment.

Possible free layer:
- identity
- observation journal
- basic learning

Possible paid layer:
- longitudinal context
- deeper interpretation
- advanced comparison / coaching
- multi-person care / shared plants

Business model remains unproven.

## Why Plantupia may have a right to build it

Plantupia would need to convert real-world horticultural care and repeated outcomes into better teaching / interpretation.

Without a unique body of living-space knowledge, this becomes another generic plant app.

## Direct references / category baseline

Plant-care apps such as Planta and Greg demonstrate that reminders, identification, diagnosis and schedules are already common category expectations.

These features alone are not a thesis.

## Lateral references

### Merlin Bird ID — Cornell Lab
https://merlin.allaboutbirds.org/

Why it matters:
Merlin uses machine learning to help a person notice and identify what is already in the environment. Cornell explicitly frames it as a knowledgeable friend whose suggestions help the user learn rather than an infallible authority.

Useful principle:
> augmentation can increase human perception instead of replacing it.

### Seek by iNaturalist
https://www.inaturalist.org/pages/seek_app

Why it matters:
The camera sends attention outward into living nature and helps the user learn organisms encountered in the real world.

## What this world must not become

- generic "AI plant doctor"
- notification-heavy watering calendar
- anthropomorphic "your baby is sad" language as the core experience
- dependency machine where the user never learns
- gamification replacing biological reward
- fake diagnostic certainty from one image

## Smallest useful experiment

A seven-day prototype around 2–3 plants.

No full care system.

Only:

1. baseline photo / context
2. one daily or event-driven observation prompt
3. compare to previous state
4. user performs one physical check
5. system gives cautious interpretation
6. later result is recorded

Success question:

> After a week, is the person better at noticing the plant without the product?

## Kill conditions

Kill or demote this world if:

- users primarily want outsourcing rather than learning
- the learning relationship is not strong enough to support retention
- Plantupia cannot build materially better knowledge than generic models
- camera / manual observation cannot create useful enough context without burdensome input
