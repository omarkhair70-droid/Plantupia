# Source Atlas 01 — Plant × Space

Status: CLOSED FOR FIRST SYNTHESIS / REOPENABLE  
Date: 2026-10-07

## 1. Plant and environment are one design problem

Plant health cannot be understood from species alone.

Relevant context includes:

- light quantity and direction
- temperature
- humidity
- water availability
- drainage
- substrate
- HVAC exposure
- physical access
- human traffic
- maintenance capacity

Working relationship:

> living thing ↔ environment

## 2. Fit before beauty

A visually desirable plant may be a poor choice for a specific location.

A serious spatial system must therefore consider environmental fit before visual preference alone.

This does not reduce design to engineering.

It creates a tension:

> aesthetic intent × biological viability

## 3. Installation is not closure

For living design, the installation photograph is not the final proof.

A composition may:

- grow
- thin out
- lean toward light
- be pruned
- be replaced
- become denser
- fail
- recover

Working relationship:

> design ↔ time

The design is only successful if it can remain successful as a living system.

## 4. Greenery can create boundaries without walls

Plants can form:

- partial visual privacy
- soft partitions
- zone transitions
- thresholds
- spatial cues

This is different from both completely open space and hard architectural separation.

Working relationship:

> greenery ↔ boundary

## 5. Greenery can change perceived scale

Layered planting, planters and green structures can make large spaces feel more human.

The intervention may change the perceived scale of:

- atriums
- terraces
- lobbies
- open offices
- hospitality spaces

Working relationship:

> greenery ↔ human scale

## 6. Greenery can become furniture / architecture

References such as Naava and Green Furniture Concept show that the categories can merge:

plant × furniture × partition × service × technology.

The important insight is not a specific object form.

It is that planting does not have to remain a decorative layer added after spatial design.

## 7. Living systems may perform architecturally

Depending on the system, planting may contribute to:

- visual separation
- acoustic behavior
- circulation
- shade / light modulation
- spatial identity
- human-scale composition

Guardrail:

Do not generalize "plants improve acoustics" or similar claims without system-specific evidence.

## 8. Care access is part of spatial design

A living installation that is difficult to access, inspect, prune, water, repair or replace is poorly designed operationally.

Maintenance is therefore not a post-design inconvenience.

It is one of the design inputs.

Working relationship:

> spatial authorship ↔ care access

## 9. Form changes after the designer leaves

Unlike a static object, the living composition continues changing after handover.

This gives Plantupia a different design problem from ordinary decoration:

> form ↔ growth ↔ intervention ↔ time
