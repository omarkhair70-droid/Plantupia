# R1 — Market / Reference Landscape

Status: ACTIVE  
Date: 2026-10-07

## Purpose

This document is not a competitor list.

It maps different reference territories that reveal how living systems can become products, services, tools, subscriptions, or operational infrastructure.

## Territory A — Nature / greenery as a service

### Naava
https://www.naava.io/

Why it matters:

- greenery is sold as an ongoing service, not only a purchased object
- physical product, installation, maintenance and technology are integrated
- green walls can function as furniture and spatial dividers
- recurring commercial relationships are central
- architects and designers are treated as an upstream distribution channel

Take:
- physical + service + digital can be one product
- the designer can enter before procurement

Do not copy:
- Naava's exact wall product
- visual identity
- subscription structure without local validation

## Territory B — Plant sensing / connected plant

### FYTA
https://fyta.de/

Why it matters:

- plant sensing can serve both individuals and professional operators
- moisture, light, temperature and other signals become useful only through context
- API / dashboard layers allow the same physical sensing core to support different products

Take:
- one technology core may support B2C and B2B surfaces

Do not assume:
- Plantupia should manufacture sensors
- more sensors equal more product value

## Territory C — Professional interiorscape operations

### One Big Green
https://www.onebiggreen.com/

Why it matters:

- site and plant inventory
- service scheduling
- photos and service proof
- replacements / warranties
- field team workflows
- account cost and profitability

Take:
- these features are category plumbing, not innovation by themselves
- service work becomes more valuable when its evidence is visible

## Territory D — Persistent context and memory

### ORTH
https://www.askorth.com/

Why it matters:

ORTH's deeper lesson is not "AI for agriculture."

The useful structural idea is:

- a plot has persistent context
- actions and decisions accumulate
- history remains when people change
- intelligence becomes stronger because it is grounded in retained context

Take:
- memory before intelligence

Do not copy:
- farm / plot product grammar literally
- chat as the primary interface by default

## Territory E — Local / regional IoT infrastructure

### ReNile
https://renile-iot.com/

Why it matters:

- external sensor infrastructure can already exist
- proving a sensor hypothesis does not require inventing custom electronics immediately
- Plantupia can test value before manufacturing hardware

## Territory F — Consumer plant care

Examples reviewed:
- Planta: https://getplanta.com/
- Greg: https://greg.app/

Category baseline:

- identification
- reminders
- care schedules
- diagnosis
- light / watering guidance
- subscription

Conclusion:

A generic "AI plant doctor + reminders" is not a defensible thesis by itself.

It may remain a feature inside a more differentiated relationship.

## Territory G — Living data as art / atmosphere

References reviewed include plant-signal and responsive-environment installations such as:

- Perach: https://ronentanchum.art/artworks/perach.html
- Root Note: https://www.rebeccalieberman.com/root-note
- Framing Nature: https://www.spatial-forces.com/work/framing-nature
- Koishi: https://www.noelzahra.com/work/koishi

Why they matter:

They demonstrate that data does not have to become a dashboard.

Signal may become:
- sound
- light
- atmosphere
- subtle physical response
- spatial behavior

Take:
- data can become perceptible without demanding continuous attention

## Territory H — Building lifecycle / digital twin thinking

General reference territory:
- BIM / facility management
- operational digital twins
- asset history

Useful relationship:

A representation matters only when reality can update it.

A beautiful floor plan that stops changing after installation is not a living operational model.

## Current market observation

The interesting opportunity may exist between categories rather than inside one existing category:

physical green-space design × recurring care × retained history × sensing × spatial experience × human attention.

This remains an exploration field, not a chosen business.
