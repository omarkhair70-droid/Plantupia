# Source Atlas 04 — Plants as Living Systems

Status: CLOSED FOR FIRST SYNTHESIS / REOPENABLE  
Date: 2026-10-07

## Purpose

Understand plant sensing and response without reducing plants to passive decor or drifting into pseudoscience.

## 1. Plants continuously sense their environment

Plants respond to environmental conditions including:

- light
- gravity
- mechanical force
- water availability
- temperature
- injury
- chemical conditions

Working relationship:

> living organism ↔ continuous environmental sensing

## 2. Growth can encode environmental conditions

Plant form is partly shaped by what happened around it.

Examples include:

- growth toward light
- orientation relative to gravity
- mechanical-response changes under repeated force

Working relationship:

> environment → biological response → visible form

## 3. Mechanical interaction has consequences

Touch, bending, wind and other forces can influence plant growth responses.

Important distinction:

This does not mean "plants feel touch like humans."

It means mechanical stimuli can be biologically sensed and can alter plant behavior.

## 4. Local events can create wider responses

Plants use internal signaling mechanisms that may involve:

- electrical membrane changes
- calcium signaling
- chemical signaling
- hydraulic change

An event in one part of a plant may influence responses elsewhere.

Guardrail:

Electrical signaling does not imply a human-like nervous system.

## 5. Stress is not binary

A plant may:

- compensate
- regulate
- acclimate
- enter stress
- incur damage
- recover

Working relationship:

> healthy / sick is often too coarse

## 6. Biological time is environmental

Plant rhythms are tied to environmental cycles such as:

- light / dark
- temperature
- water availability
- growth stage
- season

Working relationship:

> plant time ≠ human calendar time

## 7. Past conditions may affect future response

Research on stress priming / stress memory shows that prior exposure can alter later physiological response under some conditions.

This is biological memory in a specific scientific sense.

Do not translate it into human memory or consciousness.

## 8. Plants exist in communities

Plants release and receive chemical cues within ecosystems.

Guardrail:

Avoid simplistic marketing language such as "plants talk to each other" unless the mechanism is explained accurately.

## 9. Plant intelligence is a contested term

There is real academic debate about plant cognition and intelligence.

For this project, safer language is currently:

- sensing
- signaling
- adaptation
- regulation
- living response

Do not build a scientific claim around plant consciousness.

## 10. Technology as translation

A powerful reframing emerged:

> The sensor is not what makes the plant intelligent.

The plant already senses and regulates biologically.

Technology may instead help humans read a small part of a living system that is otherwise difficult to perceive.

Working relationship:

> technology ↔ translation of living state
