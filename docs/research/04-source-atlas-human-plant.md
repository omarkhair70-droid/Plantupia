# Source Atlas 03 — Human × Plant

Status: CLOSED FOR FIRST SYNTHESIS / REOPENABLE  
Date: 2026-10-07

## 1. Presence is not relationship

Seeing a plant and caring for a plant are different experiences.

Research reviewed during this pass suggests that active interaction with plants may be experienced differently from purely digital or passive tasks.

Working relationship:

> plant presence ≠ plant relationship

## 2. Care can create attachment

Attachment does not always exist before care.

It may grow through:

- watering
- pruning
- repotting
- rescuing a declining plant
- watching new growth
- learning its patterns

Working relationship:

> care → observation → response → attachment

## 3. Care is also burden

Plant care can be:

- meaningful
- calming
- satisfying
- repetitive
- annoying
- anxiety-producing
- an unwanted responsibility

This creates a core tension:

> meaningful involvement ↔ burden

## 4. Automation is not automatically better

If meaningful physical care is part of the relationship, full automation may remove value in some contexts.

The right level of automation depends on the situation:

- hospitality lobby may need high automation
- professional maintenance may need efficient exception handling
- a consumer hobbyist may value direct involvement

Working rule:

> automate the burden, not the relationship

## 5. Care is partly learning to notice

Over time people may learn to recognize:

- leaf posture
- growth pattern
- drying behavior
- color change
- direction toward light
- recovery after intervention

A strong product may teach attention rather than merely issue commands.

Working relationship:

> care ↔ literacy

## 6. Feedback is delayed

Plant interaction operates on different timescales from normal digital interfaces.

Action may occur now while visible response comes later.

Working relationship:

> action → waiting → biological response

## 7. Growth is already a reward system

A new leaf, recovery, stronger roots or successful propagation can be more meaningful than artificial points or badges.

Guardrail:

Do not gamify away the natural feedback loop.

## 8. Plants can participate in home-making

Plants may help people make a space feel:

- personal
- stable
- inhabited
- connected to memory
- connected to another person

## 9. Plants can carry social memory

Examples include:

- gifts
- inherited plants
- propagated cuttings
- plants linked to relationships or life stages

Working relationship:

> living object ↔ social memory

## 10. Physical handling matters

The plant is not only information to be read.

Relevant interactions may include:

- touching soil
- holding leaves
- pruning
- repotting
- moving
- cleaning
- propagating

Technology should not automatically separate the hand from the living thing.
