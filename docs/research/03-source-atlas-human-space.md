# Source Atlas 02 — Human × Space × Greenery

Status: CLOSED FOR FIRST SYNTHESIS / REOPENABLE  
Date: 2026-10-07

## 1. Nature is not only an image

Biophilic experience can involve:

- sight
- sound
- smell
- movement
- light
- airflow
- material texture
- changing spatial enclosure

Working relationship:

> nature experience ≠ green visual styling

## 2. Greenery can tune exposure

Spatial experience includes the balance between:

- openness
- visibility
- refuge
- privacy
- orientation

Planting can sometimes change these relationships without creating a hard wall.

This makes greenery part of spatial psychology, not only decoration.

## 3. Composition matters

The same approximate quantity of greenery can create different emotional and spatial experiences depending on:

- species
- density
- vertical structure
- texture
- visual rhythm
- cultural language
- placement

Working relationship:

> amount of green ≠ quality of experience

## 4. Greenery can choreograph movement

Planting, planters, seating and living dividers can:

- imply routes
- create pauses
- establish meeting pockets
- slow movement
- frame entry
- differentiate zones

Working relationship:

> living form ↔ human movement

## 5. Human-scale correction

Large commercial environments can feel visually cold or oversized.

Greenery may provide intermediate layers between the building scale and the body.

Working relationship:

> architecture scale ↔ living scale ↔ human body

## 6. Multisensory evidence has boundaries

Research suggests that some biophilic interventions can influence perceived restoration, satisfaction, stress and related outcomes.

However:

- results vary by study and intervention
- not every outcome improves
- self-report measures have limits
- "more plants = X% productivity" is not justified as a universal claim

Working rule:

> design boldly, claim carefully

## 7. Operational reality constrains atmosphere

In hospitality and workplaces, planting must coexist with:

- service paths
- cleaning
- safety
- access
- maintenance
- lighting
- HVAC
- guest / employee circulation

A spatial concept that cannot survive operation is not complete.
